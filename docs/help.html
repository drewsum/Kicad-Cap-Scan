CapScan user guide

Where to put stitching capacitors across plane splits.

In three steps

  1. Open the board in KiCad's PCB editor and click the CapScan button. It shows the board as read (stackup, planes and routing, the nets you pick highlighted) and lists its signal nets under Nets to scan. Tick Scan all signal nets, or type in the filter box (USB, DDR*, CLK?), tick the nets you want (Select shown ticks all the filter shows), and click Scan board. The scan refills the zones first, unless you untick that. Your picks are remembered for the board, and the next time it scans them straight away.
  2. Read the Stitching Caps tab. Each row is a part to add: its value, voltage rating, the two nets it bridges, where it goes (X / Y in KiCad's board coordinates, the side, the rotation), and the signals it returns current for.
  3. Click Place markers to see the spots on User.Comments in KiCad, then Export PDF or Export HTML for the report.

The tabs

3D Heat MapEach reference plane, with the density of return-path breaks rendered on it. Plane edges are outlined in their net's color. Signals near breaks are colored by heat, and the new capacitors are cyan blocks. Drag to turn, scroll to zoom, and use the check boxes to hide layers. The Heat tick boxes, here and on the 2D tab, pick which breaks add heat: splits, voids, vias that need a cap, and vias still missing a return via. The number by each is how many there are.
2D PlacementOne reference layer at a time:
  • planes filled by net, and the signals that return on that layer;
  • split crossings (×), voids (◇) and vias that change plane (○);
  • new capacitors (■, labeled CS#) and return vias (●, SV#);
  • existing capacitors (□): cyan when credited, amber when they are decoupling caps a return would otherwise use.
Click a marker to select it.
Stitching CapsThe parts to add.
SignalsEvery signal that loses its return path somewhere, with its routing layers, the planes it returns on, and the parts that fix it.
CrossingsEvery split and void under a trace, with the nets either side, the gap, the dielectric height, and how it is fixed.
Via TransitionsEvery via that moves a signal onto a different reference plane, and whether it needs a capacitor or a return via.
Existing CapsCapacitors already between two plane nets. Those next to an IC pin are decoupling caps and are never counted as stitching caps.

Double-click a row to find it on the map, aim the 3D view at it, and select its net (or part) in KiCad.

What the words mean

SplitThe trace runs over a gap between two plane nets (e.g. +3V3 and +1V8 on one layer). Fix: a stitching capacitor between those nets, next to the crossing.
VoidThe trace runs over no plane copper at all. A capacitor cannot help. Reroute the trace or fill the plane. A void on one side of a stripline whose other plane is solid is listed as other plane: that plane carries the return.
Via changeA via takes the signal to a layer whose reference plane is another net (needs a capacitor) or another layer of the same net (needs a return via).
Severity1 when the broken plane is the signal's only reference; 0.5 for a stripline whose other plane is solid there.
HeatHow many breaks of the kinds ticked are close together, from 0 to 1 across the board.

Settings

Apart from the nets, nothing needs setting. Four switches at the top of the Scan panel turn each kind of check on or off: split crossings, vias onto another net's plane, vias onto another plane of the same net, and voids. A check that is off is not looked for, and the summary and reports say so. The panel also has the reach of a capacitor, the signals one capacitor may serve, and the part. Advanced has every other rule: what counts as a split, the decoupling test, the voltage rating, nets to ignore (TP*) and nets to treat as planes. The reasoning and sources for each default are in docs/research.md.

Markers

Place markers draws, on User.Comments, in one undo step:

  • a circle and a line across the split for each capacitor, labeled [CapScan] CS3 100nF 16V;
  • a circle for each return via;
  • a cross on each void.

Clear markers removes only what CapScan drew.

When something is wrong

  • "Could not connect to KiCad": check Preferences → Plugins → Enable KiCad API, and that a board is open in the PCB editor.
  • No reference layers found: the zones are probably not filled. Press B in KiCad, or leave Refill zones before scanning ticked.
  • Each session's log is in your temp folder under capscan. About shows the path.
